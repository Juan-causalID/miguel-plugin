Verified verbatim against Hernan & Robins *Causal Inference: What If* (21nov25 PDF). Page refs below are PRINTED book page numbers (PDF index in parens). Used for the Clalit BSI grace-period CCW per-protocol design (cefuroxime+metro vs ceftriaxone+metro, 24h grace, 60d mortality). Companion to [[cef-metro-itt-design]] (the simpler ITT effort) and [[stabilized-weights-for-dynamic-regimes-what-if-ch-21]].

**Cloning for non-unique strategy assignment** - Ch 22.4 "Time zero", book p.316 (PDF 324): "create two exact copies-clones-of each of these individuals in the data and assign each of the two clones to a different strategy. Clones are then censored at the time their data stop being consistent with the arm they were assigned to." Assigning by the strategy a person ended up following = the "starting follow-up after randomization is potentially biased ... deaths during the first two years ... excluded" immortal-time/selection argument (Ch 22.4, book p.315).

**Grace periods** - Fine Point 22.5, book p.317 (PDF 325): grace period redefines strategies as "initiate within [window]"; data consistent with >1 strategy during the window; "Clones are censored when their data are no longer compatible with their assigned strategy ... The potential bias introduced by censoring can be handled via IP weighting." Also: "When using grace periods with cloning and censoring, the intention-to-treat effect cannot be estimated because almost everyone will contribute a clone to each of the treatment strategies ... analyses with grace period at baseline are geared towards estimating some form of per-protocol effect and thus will generally need to incorporate adequate adjustment." Timing-of-initiation-within-window must be specified (cites Cain et al. 2010 Appendix).

**Double allocation of pre-deviation events** - Ch 22.4, book p.316 (PDF 324, right after cloning passage): "if the individual had died before either clone was censored, then both clones would have died and therefore the death would have been assigned to both strategies. This double allocation of events prevents the bias that could arise if events occurring during the waiting period were systematically assigned to one of the two strategies only."

**Bootstrap re-clone** - Ch 22.4, book p.316 (PDF 324): "because individuals may be included multiple times in the analysis via their clones, appropriate adjustment of the variance of the effect estimate is required via bootstrapping." (Resample original subjects.) Ch 17 confirms CI "via bootstrapping of the individuals in the population" (book p.232, PDF 240).

**Censoring is a time-varying treatment / IPCW** - Ch 21.5 "Censoring is a time-varying treatment", book p.298-299 (PDF 306-307). Nonstabilized W_C = prod 1/Pr(C_k=0 | C_{k-1}=0, A_{k-1}, L_{k-1}); stabilized SW_C numerator = Pr(C_k=0 | C_{k-1}=0, A_{k-1}) i.e. arm + past only, NO prognostic L. "The stabilized weights do not eliminate censoring ... they make censoring occur at random ... with respect to the measured covariate history. That is, there is selection but no selection bias." Also Ch 8.5 (book p.117-120, PDF 125-128): "we can think of censoring C as just another treatment"; positivity required for Pr(remain UNCENSORED)>0 only, NOT for Pr(censored) - one-sided, exactly matching a deviation-censoring design.

**Survival CCW estimator** - Technical Point 21.10, book p.299 (PDF 307): pooled logistic model for discrete-time hazard Pr(D_{k+1}=1 | A_k, L_k, D_k=C_{k+1}=0) with time-varying nonstabilized OR stabilized W_A x W_C weights; "parameters of that model estimate the parameters of a marginal structural pooled logistic model for Pr(D^{a,c=0}_{k+1}=1 | D^{a,c=0}_k=0)". Risk = 1 - prod(1 - hazard) per Ch 17.2 (book p.230, PDF 238).

**Why report cumulative risk RD/RR not HR** - Ch 17.1 book p.228 (PDF 235): "the survival analyses in this book privilege survival/risk over hazard." Fine Point 17.2 "The hazards of hazard ratios" book p.231 (PDF 239): HR is a time-varying weighted average, "may be 1 even if the survival curves are not identical"; built-in selection bias - conditioning on collider D_1 opens A->D_1<-U->D_2 (Fig 17.3), bites even under null/in RCT. Cumulative risk "defined with respect to a fixed time period."

**Non-collapsibility - CITATION FIX**: The non-collapsibility of the ODDS RATIO is Fine Point 4.3 (book p.60, PDF 68), not TP 8.1. Technical Point 8.1 (book p.114, PDF 122) is titled "The built-in selection bias of hazard ratios"; it ALSO notes "the noncollapsibility of the hazard ratio (Greenland 1996b)" as a secondary remark. So for a hazard-based survival design: cite TP 8.1 for built-in selection bias of HR AND for HR non-collapsibility; cite Fine Point 4.3 for OR non-collapsibility. Do not cite "TP 8.1 non-collapsibility" as if it were the odds-ratio result.

**Positivity** - Fine Point 12.2 (book p.169, PDF 177) structural vs random violations + check mean of stabilized weights ~1; Technical Point 12.1 for restricting inference under structural violations. For CCW the live positivity diagnostic is on the censoring/deviation process within the window (deterministic-arm strata where everyone deviates one way = structural zero).

**Stabilized-weights-vs-dynamic caveat does NOT bite a censoring weight**: TP 21.2 (book p.284, PDF 292) "Hence stabilized weights cannot be used with dynamic strategies" applies to the TREATMENT weight SW_A when the regime is deterministic dynamic. The censoring/"no censoring" target c=0 is a STATIC intervention, so the stabilized censoring weight SW_C is explicitly endorsed (Ch 21.5, TP 21.10). A static "initiate within 24h" threshold strategy is not a covariate-driven dynamic regime, so SW_A is also fine here. See [[stabilized-weights-for-dynamic-regimes-what-if-ch-21]].
